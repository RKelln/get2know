# What to think about (not what to ask)

Use this to decide which gaps matter and what to follow up on. These are not questions to read out. Use what you already know about this kind of business or non-profit, then ask.

**Rule 1:** what is *usual* for this kind of organization tells you what to **ask**, never what to **write**. "Repair shops usually take payment at pickup" is a reason to ask how this one gets paid. It is not a fact about this one. Every line in the profile still needs `said` or `file`.

**Rule 2:** you *think like* these roles to find good questions. You never *act as* them. No tax, legal or financial advice.

## The hats

In a small organization, one or two people wear all of these hats. For each hat, ask yourself: **if someone took over this job tomorrow, what would they need to know that is not written down?** The examples show range; this organization may be like none of them.

- **Bookkeeper / accountant: money in and out.** Paid on the spot (a café), invoices and slow payers (a trades business), grants with reports owed (a non-profit), memberships (an arts centre). *Think:* where could money go missing or arrive late, and who would notice?
- **Front desk: how people reach them.** Walk-in, phone, email, social media, a booking app. Regulars or one-time visits. *Think:* what do people ask again and again, and what happens when no one answers?
- **Operations / scheduler: where the time goes.** Hands-on work by day, admin at night. Seasonal rushes (garden care in spring, tax help in March, a summer camp). *Think:* what eats hours every week, and what only breaks once a year?
- **Marketer: who they serve and who they could serve.** New services people keep asking about, who else nearby offers them, how people hear about them. *Think:* what are customers asking for that they cannot yet offer?
- **Compliance: rules they must follow.** Food safety, client or health records, working with children, alcohol, tax, a funder's terms, donor privacy. *Think:* what could get them in trouble if a tool got it wrong?
- **People / volunteer coordinator: who else is involved.** Staff, volunteers, a board, family, contractors. *Think:* who would be affected by a change, and who would need to say yes?
- **Records keeper: where information lives.** Paper, a spreadsheet, an app, an inbox, someone's memory. *Think:* what would a helper need to read, and can it? (Never ask for passwords.)
- **Owner / director: what the point is.** The personal phone call, the artist's eye, the regular who gets a free coffee, the counsellor's judgment. *Think:* what must stay human, even if a tool could do it faster?

## Picking gaps

Rank a gap higher when the person in one of these hats, or an AI helper doing that job, would give worse or riskier help without the answer: unknown rules, unknown limits, unknown money flows, unknown "must stay human" tasks. Rank it lower when it is nice to know but would not change any idea.
