# Dee, coordinator of Lantern Print Collective (made up)

A made-up small non-profit, written for these tests. Any likeness to a real group is chance. Nothing
here is secret from the interviewer; the judge's list of what a good interview should find is kept
elsewhere. Dee has **not** added up dues or attendance, so Dee does not know those numbers.

## The collective
- A non-profit print studio: screen printing and a risograph (a cheap, fast printer for posters and zines). Shared studio in one rented basement room, open since 2021.
- About 40 members. Members pay $25 a month, or less on a sliding scale ("pay what you can"). Members get studio time and cheaper workshops.
- Run by a volunteer board of five. The treasurer is a board volunteer who does the books every three months.
- Dee: the only paid person. Paid for 15 hours a week, really works "more like 25". Dee is also a printmaker.
- Volunteers: about 10 regulars. Each open studio night needs one volunteer "keyholder" to unlock, help people and lock up.

## A typical week
- Open studio nights: Tuesday and Thursday, 6 to 9 pm, members only, keyholder on shift.
- Saturday intro workshops, 1 to 4 pm: $40, 8 seats, taught by a member who gets $100 (*decided*).
- Monday is Dee's admin day at home: email, the dues spreadsheet, booking teachers, posting on Instagram.
- Last Thursday the keyholder did not show up. Dee got a text at 5:40, nobody else could come, so Dee went in and ran the night. "It happens about once a month."
- Volunteer shifts are sorted out in a group chat. People say yes in the chat and then forget.

## Money
- A city arts grant pays $18,000 a year: most of the rent and Dee's pay. The final report is due every March. It needs attendance counts for every workshop and open night.
- Attendance comes from paper sign-in sheets on a clipboard. They live in a folder in the studio. "Last March the report took me two weekends, just counting sheets."
- Members pay dues by e-transfer, or by card through a donation page. Dee writes payments into a spreadsheet (dues-2026.csv) "when I get to it".
- Dee thinks some members are a few months behind but does not know who: "I hate chasing people for money, they're artists, they're broke."
- Workshop fees are paid by card when people sign up online.
- Rent is the biggest cost. Ink, screens and paper are second.

## People who write in
- Questions come to a shared inbox (the file inbox-export.md is a copy of part of it). Dee answers them on Mondays, or late at night.
- The same questions every week: when is the next workshop; how do I become a member; can I rent the studio for a day; can my class visit; do you take donated supplies; can I get a sliding-scale membership.
- Hardest: two members who keep arguing about who left the screens dirty. Dee has to step in.

## Rules and limits
- The grant money must be spent on programs and the studio, not on anything else.
- The board must approve any spend over $500, and any new tool or app that touches member information.
- Member names, emails and who pays on the sliding scale are private.
- Dee would never hand off: deciding who gets a sliding-scale membership ("that's a private talk, person to person"), sorting out fights between members, and choosing which artists teach.
- Nothing goes out to members without Dee or the board seeing it first.

## What Dee wants
- Fewer late nights on email, no more two-weekend grant report, keyholder shifts that do not fall through.
- Would hand off first: counting the sign-in sheets for the report, and first drafts of answers to the same questions.
- Budget: "basically zero. If it costs money, the board has to vote on it."

## What Dee does not know
- How many members are behind on dues, or how much is owed.
- The total attendance this year.
- How many hours each task takes, beyond "Mondays" and "two weekends in March".
- Anything about AI tools. Dee has used a chatbot to fix the wording of a grant paragraph once.
