## The shop
- One-room bike repair shop on a Toronto side street, open since 2024. Regular bikes only, no e-bikes. No pickup or delivery.
- Open Tue, Wed, Fri, Sat 10 to 6; Thu 10 to 8. Closed Sunday and Monday. Monday is "ordering, invoices and email".
- Sam: owner, mechanic, bookkeeper, receptionist. About 60 hours a week. "I'm good with bikes, I'm bad with paperwork."
- Lena: part-time mechanic, three days a week (Thursday, Friday, Saturday, *decided*).
- Nobody else works there. An accountant does the year-end taxes; Sam drops off a box of receipts in March (*decided*).
- Spring is the rush (turnaround can be 5 days in April and May), summer steady, winter slow.

## A busy Saturday (*decided*)
- A line at the door at 10. The phone rings while Sam is talking to someone. Lena works on bikes in the back.
- When a bike comes in: Sam writes a paper tag (name, phone, what's wrong, a rough price) and hangs it on the bike.
- At pickup: Sam reads the tag, charges on the card terminal, and the tag goes in a shoebox.

## Money
- Most people pay at pickup: debit, credit, cash or e-transfer. Business accounts get 30 days.
- One courier company with a fleet of bikes pays "whenever", usually 6 or 7 weeks late. Sam emails them when Sam remembers, maybe once a month (*decided*).
- On Mondays Sam types the paper tags into a spreadsheet as invoices. The courier's invoices go out by email from that spreadsheet (*decided*).
- No accounting software. Sam looks at the bank app now and then but does not match payments to invoices. Sam suspects some payments do not match but "never has time to check".
- Biggest costs: rent ($2,400 a month) and parts from Northgate Cycle Supply.
- Sam thinks tune-ups bring in the most money, but has never added it up (*decided*).
- 13% HST on everything.

## Customers
- Mostly neighbourhood commuters and families, plus the courier company. One office asked about bringing in six bikes.
- People reach the shop by email (a shared inbox), phone, and walking in. No online booking. Sam texts people when a bike is ready (*decided*). People still email "is my bike ready?" before the text comes, mostly when Sam gave no date at drop-off, or the date passed with no word; Sam usually gives a date only if asked (*decided*, 2026-10-30).
- "I answer the same ten questions at 10pm." The usual ones: how much is a tune-up or a flat; can you fit me in this week; is my bike ready; do you do e-bikes; can you pick up my bike; what's the warranty; how long before storage fees; do you price match or give student discounts.
- Hardest: people who are angry their bike is late. One person said they would post a bad review (*decided* from the inbox).

## The market
- People ask about e-bikes every week in summer. Some ask about pickup and delivery. Sam does not know how many people nearby ride e-bikes, or who else nearby offers either. Sam has no van and no e-bike training.

## Older numbers (*decided*)
- The basic tune-up was $79 when the shop opened; it is $89 now.
- Lena started at two days a week; she has worked three days a week for a while now.
- Storage used to be $3 a day after 10 days; now it is $5 a day after 14 days' notice.
- The shop used to close at 6 every day; Thursday now runs to 8.

## Promises and rules
- 30-day warranty on labour; parts carry the maker's warranty. $20 deposit on parts orders. $5 a day storage after 14 days' notice.
- Customer names and emails are on file.
- Never sent without Sam seeing it: anything about money someone owes, and any reply to a complaint (*decided*).

## What Sam wants
- Less paperwork, faster answers to customers, a view on e-bikes and pickup.
- Would hand off first: the "is my bike ready" and price emails, and the Monday typing (*decided*).
- Would never hand off: deciding what is wrong with a bike, talking to an upset customer, anything about Lena's pay (*decided*).
- Budget: "a few hundred dollars a year, not a monthly bill I forget about" (*decided*). **Open to automation (*decided* for this case; say it only when asked what may go out on its own, or what you would hand off):** "If it's simple stuff, like hours or prices or whether a bike's ready, I don't need to see every one, as long as it's right and it says it's automatic." Wants a list of what went out. Never on its own: anything about money someone owes, any reply to a complaint, any promise of a date Sam has not set.
